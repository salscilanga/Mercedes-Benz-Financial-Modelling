# Mercedes-Benz Financial Modelling & Valuation

## Project Overview

I developed this financial model to analyse Mercedes-Benz's financial performance and estimate its equity value using financial forecasts through 2029.

The project combines historical financial information, operating assumptions, cash flow projections and valuation analysis. My aim was to understand how changes in profitability, cash flow generation and discount rates affect the value of the business.

I used a sum-of-the-parts approach because Mercedes-Benz operates both an industrial business (Cars and Vans) and a financial services business.

## Financial Model Structure

The Excel workbook contains the following worksheets:

- **Historical:** Historical financial information used as a starting point.
- **Assumptions:** Key inputs and assumptions supporting the forecasts.
- **Operating Model:** Revenue and operating performance by business segment.
- **P&L Forecast:** Projected revenue, profitability and operating margins.
- **Cash Flow:** Cash flow calculations and separate industrial cash flow assumptions.
- **DCF Valuation:** Discounted cash flow calculations, equity valuation and sensitivity analysis.
- **Dashboard:** Financial KPIs, forecasts and valuation charts.
- **Summary:** Main valuation assumptions and results.

## Valuation Methodology

I valued the industrial and financial services businesses separately.

For the industrial business, I used a discounted cash flow approach based on projected industrial free cash flows from 2026 to 2029, a terminal growth rate of 2% and a discount rate of 9.5%.

For Financial Services, I applied a price-to-book multiple of 1.47 to its book equity.

The industrial enterprise value was then combined with industrial net liquidity and the estimated value of Financial Services to calculate total equity value.

I also performed sensitivity analysis using different WACC and terminal growth assumptions to understand how these variables influence the estimated share price.

## Investment Conclusion

Based on my valuation, Mercedes-Benz has an estimated value of €146.38 per share in the base case, compared with a reference market price of €41.28. This represents a potential upside of approximately 255%.

The valuation suggests a substantial difference between the estimated value and the market price. However, I would treat this result with caution, particularly given the sensitivity of the model to future cash flow assumptions and discount rates.

The analysis helped me better understand the relationship between operating performance, cash flow generation and equity valuation. It also highlighted how much the final result can depend on the assumptions used.

## Model Limitations

The industrial cash flow projections are based on scenario assumptions rather than a fully reconciled unlevered free cash flow forecast. The industrial FCF figures used for valuation are therefore different from the cash flows calculated in the consolidated operating model.

The model uses industrial cash flow assumptions as a proxy in a WACC-based DCF. This is a methodological simplification, and the resulting enterprise value should be treated as indicative rather than a fully reconciled FCFF valuation.

The analysis also relies on assumptions about future profitability, terminal growth and the valuation multiple applied to Financial Services.

For these reasons, I consider the project primarily an exercise in financial modelling, forecasting and valuation analysis rather than an investment recommendation.
